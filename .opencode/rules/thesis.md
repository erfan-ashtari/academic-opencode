# Thesis Writing Rules

## File Handling

**Convert PDFs to .md for editing and analysis:**

```bash
/convert-document <thesis_chapter.pdf>
```

Store in the same directory as the original.

## Structure

### Master's Thesis
1. **Title Page** — University template
2. **Abstract** — 150-300 words
3. **Acknowledgments**
4. **Table of Contents**
5. **Chapter 1: Introduction** — Problem, purpose, research questions, significance
6. **Chapter 2: Literature Review** — Comprehensive review organized thematically
7. **Chapter 3: Methodology** — Research design, participants, instruments, analysis
8. **Chapter 4: Results** — Findings organized by research question
9. **Chapter 5: Discussion** — Interpretation, implications, limitations, future work
10. **References**
11. **Appendices** — Surveys, interview protocols, supplementary data

### PhD Dissertation
- Same structure but more extensive
- Multiple studies/chapters may be warranted
- Must demonstrate original contribution to knowledge
- Committee approval required at each stage
- Typically 200-300 pages

## Style Guidelines
- Use first person ("I found..." not "the researcher found...")
- Past tense for completed actions, present tense for general truths
- Avoid contractions in formal writing
- Define all acronyms on first use
- Use active voice when possible
- Maintain consistent terminology throughout

## Common Pitfalls
- Literature review that's a summary, not a synthesis
- Methods section that doesn't justify choices
- Discussion that simply restates results
- Missing limitations section
- Inconsistent citation formatting
- Too much background, not enough original contribution
- Figures/tables not referenced in text

## Committee Management
- Schedule regular check-ins (monthly minimum)
- Share drafts well before deadlines
- Take notes on feedback and track changes
- Address all reviewer comments in revision
- Use `/review-paper` for self-assessment before committee review

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Research questions are clearly stated
- [ ] Literature review is comprehensive and synthesized
- [ ] Methodology is justified and reproducible
- [ ] Results answer the research questions
- [ ] Discussion interprets findings in context of literature
- [ ] Limitations are acknowledged
- [ ] All claims are cited
- [ ] Citations are verified
- [ ] Formatting matches university requirements
- [ ] Committee feedback addressed
